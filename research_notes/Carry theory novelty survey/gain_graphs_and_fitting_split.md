# Gain/voltage graphs, covers and the free/tied (Fitting) split in carry theory: prior-work survey

Scope: novelty of three components of the Lean 4 carry-theory development:
(a) carry networks as positive-ratio gain graphs (place value = potential, frustration = non-trivial holonomy, voltage/derived cover unrolling a frustrated ring into a periodic mixed-radix "helix");
(b) the free/tied split of M = |B - c| (Fitting decomposition of multiplication-by-c on Z/M, plus CRT idempotent "branches");
(c) the canonical split (tied part = product of p^{v_p(M)} over primes p | gcd(M, c); the free part is forced).

Method note: several primary hosts (arxiv.org, combinatorics.org, en.wikipedia.org, pubs.lib.umn.edu) were unreachable from this sandbox (DNS/proxy 403), so some findings rest on search-result snippets rather than full-text reads. These are marked "(snippet)". Zaslavsky's DS8 bibliography could not be downloaded or grepped, so its coverage of arithmetic and carry applications is still unchecked (see Gaps). Mathlib facts were checked against the local checkout used by this repo (mathlib4 commit 79a359dfe4682c5ca375c39522d7c64e95659d5a).

## Q1. Balanced gain graph iff potential exists; derived covers of cycles (Zaslavsky; Gross; Gross & Tucker)

### Takeaway
The structural facts behind (a) are textbook-standard. A gain graph is balanced (every cycle has identity gain) iff it switches to identity gains, iff a potential function exists (Zaslavsky 1989, "Biased graphs I"). The derived graph of a voltage graph is a covering graph, and a cycle whose net voltage has infinite order lifts to two-way infinite paths (Gross 1974; Gross & Tucker 1987). So "place values = potential", "frustrated = unbalanced cycle" and "the ring unrolls into a helix on the derived cover" are the **same theorems**, specialised to the gain group (Q_{>0}, ·) or (R_{>0}, ·).

### Cited Findings
- Zaslavsky's balance criterion: a gain graph is balanced iff its gains switch to the identity gain. For a T-gain graph (complex unit gains) the following are equivalent: balanced, switching equivalent to (G,1), and having a potential function. — [On the adjacency matrix of a complex unit gain graph, arXiv:1812.03747](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.03747) (snippet; restates Zaslavsky 1989)
- Switching by a vertex function λ changes each edge gain by the endpoint values. Switching does not change which circles are balanced, nor whether the gain graph is balanced. — [arXiv:1812.03747](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.03747) (snippet)
- A gain graph is balanced iff every circle in a fundamental system of circles (for any spanning tree) is balanced, citing Zaslavsky (1989), Corollary 3.2. — [arXiv:1812.03747 / related restatement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.03747) (snippet)
- Original source: T. Zaslavsky, "Biased graphs. I. Bias, balance, and gains", J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 47 (1989) 32–52. The search sources above cite it as "Zaslavsky (1989)". The glossary is EJC Dynamic Survey DS9 and the bibliography is DS8 (editions 1998–2018; a preliminary 10th edition dated 2024-02-06 is on the author's homepage). — [Zaslavsky Bsg home page](https://people.math.binghamton.edu/zaslav/Bsg/index.html); [DS8 v9 (2018)](https://www.combinatorics.org/files/Surveys/ds8/ds8v9-2018.pdf); [DS9 glossary](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/DS9)
- Gross, "Voltage graphs", Discrete Math. 9 (1974) 239–246. The derived graph of a voltage graph is the covering graph obtained from the voltage assignment. Its vertex set is V × A and its edges are defined from the edge labels. Each undirected edge is treated as a pair of opposite arcs with mutually inverse labels. — [MathWorld: Voltage Graph](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/VoltageGraph.html); [MathWorld: Derived Graph](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/DerivedGraph.html) (snippet)
- Gross & Tucker also show that permutation voltage assignments generate all graph coverings. The 1977 Discrete Math. 18 paper is attributed to Gross & Tucker by MathWorld but to Tucker alone by Wikipedia, so the attribution conflicts across sources. A 2026 preprint proves a quantum-graph "Gross–Tucker theorem" characterising which graphs are derived graphs, and identifies the classical derived graph with Gross–Tucker's (Prop. 4.12). — [MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/VoltageGraph.html); [Schäfer & Tobolski, arXiv:2602.20996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20996) (snippet)
- Gross's 1974 paper "Voltage graphs" is the PJM-adjacent reference; the related PJM 1974 paper surfaced at [msp.org pjm v55 n2 p08](https://msp.org/pjm/1974/55-2/pjm-v55-n2-p08-p.pdf) (snippet only; content not verified)

### Inferences
- With multiplicative positive-real or rational gains, "consistent weighting w with w_target = rho·w_source on every edge" is exactly Zaslavsky's potential. "Frustrated" (holonomy ≠ 1) is exactly "unbalanced". The project's theorem "a weight exists iff every cycle has ratio product 1" is the **same theorem** as Zaslavsky's balance–potential equivalence (and Harary's 1953 signed-graph balance theorem in the ±1 case). The only change is notation (multiplicative gains in a torsion-free abelian group).
- The "helix" is the standard lift of a cycle in a derived graph. Recalled from Gross & Tucker, *Topological Graph Theory* (Wiley 1987; Dover 2001), §2.1, but not verified here: if a k-cycle has net voltage of order m in group A, each component of its preimage is a km-cycle, and there are |A|/m of them. In the infinite-order case (any holonomy ≠ 1 in Q_{>0}) the components are two-way infinite paths. Reading the sheets of that path as place values gives the periodic schedule b_j = rho_{j mod n}. That reading is a **re-interpretation** of a standard object; we found no paper that makes it.
- Verdict for (a), structural part: **known** (same theorems). Novelty lies only in the application and the interpretation (see Q2).

- Could not grep DS8 for "carry", "numeration", "radix", "odometer", "arithmetic" (combinatorics.org blocked). This is the single most important remaining check for (a). Recommend a manual search of the 2018 PDF or the 2024 preliminary edition.

## Q2. Prior use of gain/voltage graphs, holonomy or covers for carries, mixed radix, numeration, odometers or chip-firing

### Takeaway
We found **no** prior work that models carries, mixed-radix place values or number systems with gain graphs, voltage graphs, holonomy or covering spaces. Three nearby lines do exist: (i) "exploding dots" / Propp's a←b machines and chip-firing on a line producing fractional bases, where carrying is literally chip-firing with a ratio; (ii) arithmetical graphs, where each vertex has its own "currency" with an integer exchange rate, the closest analogue of per-edge carry ratios; (iii) odometers on numeration scales and rational base systems in symbolic dynamics. None uses balance, holonomy or a derived cover. Applying gain graphs and covers to carry networks therefore looks **possibly novel as a framing**, though the mathematics under it is standard.

### Cited Findings
- Tanton's "Exploding Dots": in a 1←10 machine ten dots in one box explode into one dot in the next box, which is ordinary base-10 carrying. In the 2←3 machine three dots become two dots one place to the left, which gives place values (3/2)^k ("base one-and-a-half"), credited to Jim Propp. — [G'Day Math, Exploding Dots ch. 9 (base 1.5)](https://gdaymath.com/lessons/explodingdots/9-2-base-one-half/); [Tanton, Exploding Dots educators' version](https://archive.mathteacherscircle.org/assets/session-materials/JTantonExplodingDots_EducatorsVersion.pdf); [gdaymath p=918](https://gdaymath.com/?p=918)
- Borodin, Han, Ji, Khovanova et al., "Chip-Firing and Fractional Bases" (arXiv:1809.09676; Minnesota J. Undergraduate Math.). They study chip-firing on the integer line suggested by Propp: a vertex with ≥ a+b chips fires a to the left and b to the right. They relate the left part of the final state to fractional base b/a and note that this "was also called exploding dots and was popularized by Tanton". — [arXiv:1809.09676](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09676); [MJUM PDF](https://pubs.lib.umn.edu/index.php/mjum/article/download/4235/2906/19697) (snippet)
- Asadi & Backman extend Baker–Norine chip-firing to arithmetical graphs by giving each vertex its own currency, with the vertex multiplicity acting as an integer exchange rate against a universal chip currency. Arithmetical structures (Lorenzini) come from intersection matrices of degenerating curves, and their critical groups are cokernel torsion of diag(d) − A. — [Asadi & Backman, arXiv:1012.0287](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.0287); [Chip-Firing Games and Critical Groups survey, arXiv:1908.04395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04395); [Arithmetical Structures on Fan Graphs, arXiv:2503.01913](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.01913) (snippets)
- Chip-firing on **signed** graphs (gain group {±1}) exists: Cho, Dochtermann, Inagaki, Oh, Snustad, Zacovic, "Chip-firing and critical groups of signed graphs", built on the reduced signed Laplacian (Guzmán–Klivans framework for invertible matrices). They compute critical groups of signed cycles, wheels, complete graphs and fans, and study the critical group under vertex switching. The authors do not use the term "gain graph" and do not discuss arithmetic. — [arXiv:2306.09315](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.09315v2); [Moore, Texas State thesis 2021](https://digital.library.txstate.edu/handle/10877/13700) (snippets)
- A "Chip-firing on graphs of groups" preprint exists (arXiv:2307.03348). We did not read it and do not know whether it involves gains or holonomy. — [arXiv:2307.03348](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03348) (title only)
- Chip-firing on directed graphs (Björner–Lovász–Shor; Holroyd, Levine, Mészáros, Peres, Propp, Wilson): the termination bound multiplies out-degrees backward along a directed path to the sink. The survey credits Engel's "probabilistic abacus" (1970s) as pedagogical origin. No numeration framing was found there. — [Holroyd et al., Chip-Firing and Rotor-Routing on Directed Graphs](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~levine/sand.pdf) (snippet); [Klivans, Chip Firing notes](https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/cklivans/Chip-Firing.pdf)
- Odometers in numeration: Barat, Downarowicz, Iwanik, Liardet, "Propriétés topologiques et combinatoires des échelles de numération", Colloq. Math. 84/85 (2000), use a rooted tree describing the carries in the addition of 1 on general numeration scales (mixed radix included). Berthé & Rigo, "Odometers on regular languages" (Theory Comput. Syst. 2007), describe carry propagation for +1 on infinite words. — [IMPAN Colloq. Math. 84/2](https://impan.pl/pl/wydawnictwa/czasopisma-i-serie-wydawnicze/colloquium-mathematicum/all/84/2/111615/proprietes-topologiques-et-combinatoires-des-echelles-de-numeration); [Berthé–Rigo, HAL](https://hal-lirmm.ccsd.cnrs.fr/lirmm-00130835/document); [Springer s00224-005-1215-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00224-005-1215-5)
- Rational base numeration: Akiyama, Frougny, Sakarovitch, "Powers of rationals modulo 1 and rational base number systems", Israel J. Math. 168 (2008) 53–91. They give integer expansions in base p/q, with the expansion language non-regular but addition computable by a letter-to-letter finite right transducer, and connect this to Mahler's 3/2 problem. — [author PDF](https://www.math.tsukuba.ac.jp/~akiyama/papers/3half_H65fullTH-060406.pdf); [Frougny PDF](https://www.irif.fr/~cf/publications/IJM.pdf)
- Hardware wrap-around carries: end-around carry gives (A+B) mod (2^n−1) = (A+B+c_out) mod 2^n, fed back in parallel-prefix adders (Zimmermann). Pseudo-Mersenne reduction for p = 2^n − c replaces subtracting p with adding c and dropping the 2^n carry, which is a carry that re-enters with gain c. — [Guyot, Modular representation (EPFL lecture notes)](https://www.ece.ucdavis.edu/~vojin/CLASSES/EPFL/Lectures/Guyot/english/Modulo.htm); [arXiv:0903.3900 (pseudo-Mersenne reduction)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.3900) (snippets)
- Targeted searches found nothing combining "gain graph" or "voltage graph" with carry propagation or mixed radix. Results were only adder patents with no graph-theoretic framing (e.g. [US10073677 mixed-radix carry-lookahead](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10073677)).

### Inferences
- The "carry ring" with lap value B and wrap gain c, computing mod B − c, generalises end-around carry (c = 1, B = 2^n) and is the same idea as pseudo-Mersenne folding (B = 2^n, c small). The congruence B ≡ c (mod B − c) is the whole content. In the project's language, the holonomy of the ring is B/c, and the cover unrolls into ordinary carries. This is **known as arithmetic** (end-around carry, Crandall/Solinas-style reduction) but **not known in the gain-graph/holonomy language**.
- Exploding dots, chip-firing on a line and arithmetical-graph currencies show that "an overflow of rho units becomes 1 unit at the next place" is known as chip-firing with exchange rates. A ring of places with a non-unit ratio product is an unbalanced multiplicative gain cycle, but none of these papers says so or uses switching, potentials or covers.
- Verdict for (a): **known in parts** (balance/potential and derived covers are standard; carry as chip-firing with ratios is standard; wrap-around carry mod B − c is standard). The **unification** (carry network = positive-ratio gain graph, place value = potential, frustration = holonomy, helix = derived-cover lift equal to a periodic mixed-radix schedule) appears **possibly novel as an explicit framework**. No machine-checked formalization of any of it was found. (Mathlib has no gain-graph, voltage-graph or covering-graph library: a grep of the local Mathlib for "gain graph", "voltage graph", "covering graph" and "holonomy" returned nothing.)

### Gaps
- DS8 (Zaslavsky's ~500-page bibliography) was not searchable here. A relevant entry could exist under "gains in economics/exchange rates" or "gain graphs in physics". Zaslavsky does annotate applications such as "consistency" and "arbitrage", but that is a recollection and was not verified.
- The well-known equivalences "consistent pairwise-comparison matrix iff weights exist" (Saaty AHP) and "no currency arbitrage iff every cycle's exchange-rate product is 1 iff prices exist" are the same theorem in other applied areas. No URLs were fetched for them in this pass.
- arXiv:2307.03348 ("Chip-firing on graphs of groups") was not read.

## Q3. Eventually periodic radix expansions / powers mod M: the classical b-adic analogue of the free/tied split

### Takeaway
The "rho" shape of B_k mod M is classical. Hardy & Wright Thm 135–136 (Ch. IX) state that 1/q in base 10 is purely periodic with period ord_q(10) when (q,10) = 1, and otherwise mixed recurring: the preperiod comes from the 2- and 5-parts of q (the primes shared with the base) and the period is the order of 10 modulo the part coprime to 10. That is exactly the free/tied split for c = 10 (tail from the tied part, cycle from the order of c on the free part). The project's (b) is therefore the **same idea and essentially the same theorem**, transposed from 1/q expansions to place values mod M.

### Cited Findings
- Hardy & Wright, *An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers*, §9.2–9.3. Theorem 135: the decimal for rational p/q in (0,1) is terminating or recurring, and conversely. If (q,10) = 1 and ν is the order of 10 (mod q), then p/q is a pure recurring decimal with period exactly ν digits, and conversely. The mixed case is set up with q = 2^α 5^β Q. — [H&W Ch. IX scan](https://www.lamfa.u-picardie.fr/sorlin/HW4.pdf) (snippet)
- The number of digits in the repeating block of 1/n is the order of 10 mod n when gcd(10,n) = 1. — [Dujella lecture notes §3.8](https://web.math.pmf.unizg.hr/%7Eduje/pdf/section3-8.pdf); [Purdue NT hw/sol 6](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~twooley/2025nt/2025nt_sol6.pdf) (snippets)
- Generalisation to Euclidean domains: "Divisibility tests and recurring decimals in Euclidean domains". — [arXiv:math/0406025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0406025) (title/snippet)

### Inferences
- Recalled but not verified: H&W Theorem 136 states that for q = 2^α 5^β Q with (Q,10) = 1, μ = max(α,β) and ν = ord_Q(10), the decimal is mixed recurring with μ non-recurring and ν recurring digits. For a general base c the preperiod is max_{p | c} ceil(v_p(q)/v_p(c)), which reduces to μ = max(α,β) for c = 10. This equals the least k with tied(q) | c^k, which is the project's "line dies after rN steps" threshold (tied part), while the period is the order of c on the free part ("helix closes after L laps").
- Pollard-rho "tail + cycle" language for the orbit of x ↦ c·x on the finite set Z/M is the generic eventually-periodic structure of any self-map of a finite set. The specific tail/cycle lengths above come from the Fitting split.
- Verdict for (b), periodicity part: **known** (same theorem up to transposition). The CRT-orthogonal-branches part is standard commutative algebra (see Q4).

### Gaps
- The verbatim text of H&W Thm 136 and a general-base statement (e.g. Niven–Zuckerman–Montgomery, *An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers*, 5th ed., §6.? on "decimal" or base-b expansions; or Rosen, *Elementary Number Theory*, §12.?) were not retrieved. Section and theorem numbers must be checked before citing.

## Q4. Fitting's lemma, the c-primary split and CRT idempotents (incl. the canonical split (c)) and existing formal libraries

### Takeaway
For multiplication by c on the finite Z-module Z/M, Fitting's lemma gives Z/M = ker(c^n) ⊕ im(c^n) for large n. The kernel part is Z/T with T the c-primary (tied) part and the image part is Z/F with F the free part; CRT supplies orthogonal idempotents e_T + e_F = 1, e_T·e_F = 0. All of this is standard algebra (Fitting 1933; Jacobson *Basic Algebra II*; primary decomposition of finite abelian groups). The canonical split (c) (T = product of p^{v_p(M)} over p | gcd(M,c), F = M/T, F forced) is the unique factorization of M into a c-smooth and a c-coprime part, which is elementary. Mathlib already contains the Fitting decomposition, ordProj/ordCompl, ZMod.chineseRemainder and CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents, so (b)–(c) are **known mathematics with existing library support**. Any novelty is in specialising them to carry moduli and connecting them to (a).

### Cited Findings
- Fitting lemma (module form): for an endomorphism φ of a module that is both artinian and noetherian, the kernels and images of φ^n stabilise and M = ker φ^n ⊕ im φ^n for large n. This applies to finite abelian groups. In the indecomposable finite-length case every endomorphism is an automorphism or nilpotent. — [Quasi-inverse endomorphisms, arXiv:1212.2554, Thm 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.2554); [Wikipedia: Fitting lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitting_lemma); [ETSU notes II.3](https://faculty.etsu.edu/gardnerr/5410/notes/II-3.pdf) (snippets)
- Mathlib (local checkout, commit 79a359df): `LinearMap.eventually_isCompl_ker_pow_range_pow [IsArtinian R M] [IsNoetherian R M] (f : Module.End R M) : ∀ᶠ n in atTop, IsCompl (ker (f ^ n)) (range (f ^ n))`, docstring "This is the Fitting decomposition of the module M with respect to the endomorphism f". Also `LinearMap.isCompl_iSup_ker_pow_iInf_range_pow`. — [Mathlib/RingTheory/Artinian/Module.lean L338](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/79a359dfe4682c5ca375c39522d7c64e95659d5a/Mathlib/RingTheory/Artinian/Module.lean#L338)
- Mathlib notations `ordProj[p] n = p ^ n.factorization p` and `ordCompl[p] n = n / ordProj[p] n` (p-part and p-coprime part). — [Mathlib/Data/Nat/Factorization/Defs.lean L262–270](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/79a359dfe4682c5ca375c39522d7c64e95659d5a/Mathlib/Data/Nat/Factorization/Defs.lean#L262)
- Mathlib `ZMod.chineseRemainder {m n} (h : m.Coprime n) : ZMod (m * n) ≃+* ZMod m × ZMod n`; structures `OrthogonalIdempotents` / `CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents` with `CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents.ringEquivOfComm` (product decomposition of a commutative ring from a complete orthogonal idempotent family). — [Mathlib/Data/ZMod/Basic.lean L874](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/79a359dfe4682c5ca375c39522d7c64e95659d5a/Mathlib/Data/ZMod/Basic.lean#L874); [Mathlib/RingTheory/Idempotents.lean](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/79a359dfe4682c5ca375c39522d7c64e95659d5a/Mathlib/RingTheory/Idempotents.lean)
- RNS / mixed-radix conversion literature uses CRT routinely, and notes that mixed-radix conversion is slowed by sequential carry propagation. — [Applying the Residue Number System to Network Inference, arXiv:1712.04614](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.04614); [UCD repository: Fermat-number RNS paper](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/entities/publication/bb064961-5801-4102-b6d8-9949c8d61ee8) (snippets)

### Inferences
- For (b): Z/M = ker(c^N) ⊕ im(c^N) with ker = (F)/M ≅ Z/T and im ≅ Z/F (for N ≥ tail length). On Z/F, c is a unit, so c^k mod F is purely periodic with period ord_F(c). On Z/T, c^N = 0. This is exactly "helix closes after L laps" and "the line dies". Products of elements in different CRT components vanish because e_T·e_F = 0. All of this is **the same theorem** as Fitting + CRT; nothing new mathematically.
- For (c): the "forced free part" says that if M = F'·T' with gcd(F',c) = 1 and every prime of T' dividing c, then T' = T and F' = F. This follows from unique factorisation and is folklore (the "c-part" or "smooth part" of an integer). It is **known**. Calling the c-primary prime-power factors "residue types" is a naming choice.
- RNS papers use CRT for representation but none found frames the CRT components as orthogonal idempotent "branches" on which products vanish. That orthogonality (e_i e_j = 0) is textbook ring theory, so the novelty is at most terminological.
- Overall verdicts: (a) known in parts, with the gain-graph/holonomy/cover framing of carries possibly novel (no prior instance found). (b) known (Fitting + CRT; same as H&W's pure/mixed recurring theorem transposed). (c) known (elementary unique factorisation). The Lean formalization connecting (a)–(c) for carry networks is plausibly new as a formal artifact. It can reuse Mathlib's Fitting decomposition, ordProj/ordCompl, CRT and CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents rather than claim these as contributions.
