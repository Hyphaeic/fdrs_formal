# Carry quotients and mixed-radix (unsaturated-limb) modular multiplication: prior-work survey

Method note (applies to all sections): in this session the sandbox proxy blocked direct downloads from ams.org, cr.yp.to, arxiv.org, eprint.iacr.org, HAL and brown.edu (HTTP 403 / DNS failure). GitHub raw *was* reachable, so the fiat-crypto statements below were checked against the actual source (`src/Arithmetic/Core.v`, `src/Arithmetic/ModOps.v`, master branch, fetched 2026-10-12). Everything else comes from search-engine summaries of the primary sources, plus items explicitly marked **[recalled, not verified this session]**, which come from background knowledge and need checking against the PDF before anyone cites them in a paper.

Verdict summary (details in each section):
- (a) Z^n / carries ≅ Z/(B - c): **known in parts.** The soundness direction ("carries preserve value mod B - c") is folklore and is machine-checked in fiat-crypto. The group-theoretic isomorphism is an elementary instance of the cokernel/critical-group computation for diag(d) - A on a (weighted) directed cycle. No source found states it as the carry-quotient theorem for mixed radices and a general wrap gain. A formal Lean proof including the completeness direction looks new as a formalization, but mathematically it is routine.
- (b) Wrapped convolution at X with X^N = c is multiplication mod X^N - c: **known.** This is standard (Crandall–Fagin weighted convolutions, Solinas/Chung–Hasan polynomial reduction, Bajard–Imbert–Plantard AMNS/PMNS with E(X) = X^n - λ, fiat-crypto `Associational.reduce`).
- (c) Product coefficients κ(m,j) = B_m B_j / B_{m+j} on product-closed mixed rings, with 25519 gains 19/2/38: **known in substance.** It is implicit in Crandall–Fagin's irrational-base weights and explicit as an algorithm in fiat-crypto's `place` (coefficient `fst t / weight i`) and in Bernstein's radix-2^25.5 field arithmetic. The abstract "product-closed" condition stated as a named hypothesis on a general mixed schedule was not found, so that packaging is possibly novel.
- (d) Landing criterion gcd(εB_k, |B - c|) | B_m B_j and "residue-closed" rings: **possibly novel.** No prior statement was found. fiat-crypto handles non-product-closed weights only by *exact* integer placement (falling back to a lower limb with a larger coefficient). It never places a term "modulo B - c" using invertibility of the wrap gain.

## Q1. Is Z^n / ⟨carry moves⟩ ≅ Z/(B − c) stated in the literature (chip-firing / critical groups / Smith normal form / pseudo-Mersenne reduction)?

### Takeaway
No source found states this exact theorem (mixed radices ρ_i, arbitrary integer wrap gain c, quotient by the carry lattice equals Z/(B − c)). It is a direct specialisation of two well-developed frameworks:
- critical groups / cokernels of generalized Laplacians diag(d) − A of digraphs, where the carry matrix is diag(ρ) minus a directed n-cycle with one arc weighted c;
- chip-firing on M-matrices / invertible integer matrices.

The cryptographic-arithmetic literature only uses the soundness half: carries and wrap-reductions preserve the value mod B − c. fiat-crypto machine-checks that half for arbitrary weights.

### Cited Findings
- **Critical group framework.** For a digraph, the critical group is the torsion part of the cokernel of the Laplacian, and the cokernel is read from the Smith normal form. — [Alfaro, Valencia, Vázquez-Ávila, "Digraphs with at most one trivial critical ideal", arXiv:1703.08621](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.08621); [Chip-Firing Games and Critical Groups, arXiv:1908.04395](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04395)
- **Critical ideals with indeterminate diagonal.** The generalized Laplacian L(D, X_D) of a digraph has indeterminates x_u on the diagonal and minus the arc multiplicities off the diagonal. The i-th critical ideal is generated by its i×i minors. — [arXiv:1703.08621](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.08621)
  - Corrales & Valencia determine generators of the critical ideals of cycles (and of complete graphs and paths). — [Corrales & Valencia, "On the critical ideals of graphs", LAA 2013, arXiv:1205.3105](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.3105)
  - Relevance: the carry matrix is exactly diag(ρ) − A, with A a directed n-cycle whose wrap arc has multiplicity c. Specialising x_i ↦ ρ_i gives the cokernel.
- **Multi-edge (undirected) cycles.** The sandpile group of "thick cycles" has been computed explicitly from gcds of minors of the Laplacian. — [EJGTA paper on thick cycles](https://ejgta.org/index.php/ejgta/article/download/1518/pdf_243)
- **Arithmetical-structure critical groups.** Defined as the torsion of coker(diag(d) − A); this is Lorenzini's arithmetical-graph setting. — [Smith Normal Form in Combinatorics (Stanley), ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301880803_Smith_Normal_Form_in_Combinatorics)
- **Chip-firing on general matrices.** Guzmán & Klivans extend chip-firing from graph Laplacians to M-matrices and to arbitrary invertible integer matrices, keeping critical/superstable/energy-minimizer theory. Equivalence classes of configurations are cosets of the lattice spanned by the firing moves. — [Guzmán & Klivans, "Chip-firing on general invertible matrices", arXiv:1508.04262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.04262); [Klivans M-chip notes](https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/cklivans/M-chip.pdf)
  - Signed-graph version: critical and z-superstable configurations are counted by det of the signed Laplacian. — [Chip-firing and critical groups of signed graphs, arXiv:2306.09315](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09315)
  - Relevance: a negative wrap gain (c = −1) is a signed arc.
- **Chip-firing as a number system.** Propp's a-b chip-firing on the integer line produces base-(b/a) representations, and "adding one chip and firing" is carrying. — [Chip-Firing and Fractional Bases, Minnesota J. Undergrad. Math.](https://pubs.lib.umn.edu/index.php/mjum/article/download/4235/2906/19697)
  - This links carries and chip-firing explicitly, but on an infinite line: no wrap, no quotient group.
- **fiat-crypto soundness lemmas (checked in source).**
  - `Associational.reduction_rule`: `(a + s*b) mod (s - c) = (a + c*b) mod (s - c)`.
  - `eval_reduce` and `eval_repeat_reduce`: wrap reduction preserves value mod `s - eval c`.
  - `Positional.eval_carry`: a carry at index i preserves `eval` exactly, assuming only `weight (S i) / weight i <> 0`.
  - `eval_carry_reduce`: preserves value mod `s - eval c`.
  - None of these states the converse (that the kernel of evaluation mod B − c *equals* the carry lattice) or any group isomorphism.
  - — [fiat-crypto Core.v](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/Core.v); [ModOps.v](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/ModOps.v)
- **Shared idea behind the reduction tricks.** Erbsen's S&P'19 slides name "a weight w with w mod m = c" as the common idea behind pseudo-Mersenne, Solinas and mixed-radix tricks. — [Erbsen S&P 2019 slides](https://www.ieee-security.org/TC/SP2019/SP19-Slides-pdfs/Andres_Erbsen_-_14-Andres_Erbsen-Simple_High-Level_Code_For_Cryptographic_Arithmetic_-_With_Proofs_Without_Compromises.pdf)
- **[recalled, not verified this session]** Crandall's pseudo-Mersenne moduli 2^q − C with reduction by multiplying the high part by C: R. E. Crandall, US Patent 5,159,632 (1992). Solinas, "Generalized Mersenne Numbers", CORR 99-39, Univ. Waterloo (1999). Both state only the reduction congruence, not a quotient-group theorem.

### Inferences
- Mathematically, (a) is a 2-line Smith-normal-form fact:
  - The carry matrix M is cyclic bidiagonal with −ρ_i on the diagonal, 1 on the subdiagonal and c in the corner.
  - det M = ±(B − c).
  - The (n−1)×(n−1) minor obtained by deleting the first row and last column is ±1, because it is triangular with 1s. So the (n−1)-th determinantal divisor is 1 and coker M is cyclic of order |B − c|.
  - In critical-ideal language, the directed cycle has n − 1 trivial critical ideals, and the n-th is ⟨∏x_i − c⟩.
  - A specialist in critical groups would therefore call (a) "known in principle / folklore". The probable novelty is (i) the explicit identification of positional carry arithmetic with this cokernel, including the explicit isomorphism given by evaluation x ↦ Σ x_i B_i mod (B − c), and (ii) a machine-checked proof of *both* directions in Lean.
- For a write-up: "carries preserve value mod B − c" is clearly known and formally verified (fiat-crypto, Coq). "The carry lattice is exactly the kernel" is the part worth stating as a contribution, with the critical-group literature cited as the conceptual antecedent.
- For c = 1 with all ρ_i equal to a base b, the result is the classical end-around-carry fact for the modulus b^n − 1 (one's-complement arithmetic). For c = −1 it is arithmetic mod b^n + 1 (Fermat/Schönhage–Strassen setting). Both are textbook folklore, but I did not locate a citable statement in quotient-group form.

- Could not retrieve Corrales–Valencia §4 (critical ideals of cycles) or Lorenzini's arithmetical-graph papers. So I cannot confirm whether a *directed* cycle with a weighted arc is treated explicitly, as opposed to undirected cycles.
- No source found that names "carry lattice" or "carry quotient" for mixed-radix positional systems. Targeted searches for chip-firing plus number-system/carry returned only the fractional-base line-chip-firing work.
- Knuth TAOCP vol. 2 §4.1 discusses mixed-radix positional notation **[recalled]**. I could not check whether it says anything about wrap-around (cyclic) mixed radix or quotient groups.

## Q2. Crandall & Fagin (1994): what exactly do they prove, and how close is it to (b)–(c)?

### Takeaway
Crandall–Fagin introduce weighted (twisted) cyclic convolutions ("DWT"), computed by FFT, so that convolution mod X^N − c-type relations (c = ±1, and twisted variants) is performed without zero-padding. Their "irrational-base DWT" (IBDWT) represents residues mod 2^q − 1 with **variable digit widths** b_j = ⌈qj/N⌉ − ⌈q(j−1)/N⌉ and weights a_j = 2^{⌈qj/N⌉ − qj/N}.

This is the closest prior art to (b)+(c) for the case c = 1 with power-of-two radices. The weights a_j are exactly a device to absorb the product coefficients κ(m,j) = B_m B_j / B_{m+j} ∈ {1, 2}, because a_m a_j / a_{m+j} = 2^{⌈qm/N⌉ + ⌈qj/N⌉ − ⌈q(m+j)/N⌉} = κ(m,j). So the weighted cyclic convolution of the weighted digits equals the mixed-radix product mod 2^q − 1.

### Cited Findings
- **Bibliographic details.** R. Crandall, B. Fagin, "Discrete weighted transforms and large-integer arithmetic", *Math. Comp.* 62 (1994), no. 205, 305–324, DOI 10.1090/S0025-5718-1994-1185244-1. — [AMS](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1994-62-205/S0025-5718-1994-1185244-1/); [ADS abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1994MaCom..62..305C/abstract); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Discrete-weighted-transforms-and-large-integer-Crandall-Fagin/46df3478ec0e9839ed97c72f9c8135c43390c147)
- **Purpose.** DWTs speed up multiplication by avoiding zero-padding. The gains are largest for arithmetic mod Fermat and Mersenne numbers. — [search summary of AMS abstract](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1994-62-205/S0025-5718-1994-1185244-1/)
- **IBDWT representation.** x = Σ_{j=0}^{N−1} x_j 2^{⌈pj/N⌉}, with weight signal a_j = 2^{⌈pj/N⌉ − pj/N} ∈ [1, 2). Modular multiplication mod 2^p − 1 becomes a weighted cyclic convolution, followed by an inverse transform, rounding and a carry pass that restores the variable-base representation.
  - In the paper, the digit sizes b_j and weights are equations (6.6)–(6.7) with run length N; the exact form of those equations was not retrieved.
  - — [Wikipedia: IBDWT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_base_discrete_weighted_transform); [HandWiki mirror](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Irrational_base_discrete_weighted_transform); [Thall, Fast Mersenne prime testing on the GPU](https://ece.northeastern.edu/groups/nucar/GPGPU4/files/thall.pdf)
- **Use and credit.** Used in Prime95/GIMPS, CUDALucas and Glucas for Lucas–Lehmer tests. Some sources also credit J. Doenias. — [rieselprime wiki](https://rieselprime.de/ziki/Irrational_base_discrete_weighted_transform); [Woltman & Kurowski, Fibonacci Quarterly](https://www.fq.math.ca/Papers1/46_47-3/Woltman_Kurowski.pdf)
- **[recalled, not verified this session]** The paper's general DWT theorem: for weights a, the weighted cyclic convolution x ∗_a y = a^{−1} · DFT^{−1}(DFT(a·x) · DFT(a·y)). With a_j = A^{j/N} this gives convolution modulo X^N − A, i.e., "right-angle"/negacyclic convolution for A = −1 (Fermat moduli 2^q + 1).
  - This is exactly claim (b) in the analytic (complex-weight) setting for c = ±1. I believe the paper treats only Mersenne/Fermat-type cases (c = ±1), not general c such as 19.
- **Related follow-up.** Crandall, Mayer, Papadopoulos, "Rapid multiplication modulo the sum and difference of highly composite numbers", *Math. Comp.* 72 (2003) 387–395. — [AMS](https://www.ams.org/mcom/2003-72-241/S0025-5718-02-01419-9/)

### Inferences
- **(b) is fully known.** "Wrapped convolution evaluated at X with X^N = c is multiplication mod X^N − c" is Crandall–Fagin's weighted convolution (c = ±1) and the polynomial-reduction viewpoint of Solinas and Chung–Hasan (general f). No novelty claim is possible.
- **(c) for c = 1, radix schedule 2^{⌈qj/N⌉}: known in substance.**
  - Crandall–Fagin's κ(m,j) ∈ {1, 2} is hidden inside the irrational weights rather than written as an integer coefficient table.
  - Their schedule ⌈qj/N⌉ is product-closed in the integer sense: ⌈a⌉ + ⌈b⌉ ≥ ⌈a+b⌉, so B_{m+j} | B_m B_j.
  - Their wrap handling (j + k ≥ N) relies on 2^q ≡ 1. That is the c = 1 analogue of the user's mixed wrapped convolution.
- What is *not* in Crandall–Fagin, as far as can be determined:
  - an integer (non-floating) statement with explicit κ coefficients;
  - general wrap gain c (e.g., 19);
  - non-power-of-two radices;
  - any treatment of schedules where B_{m+j} ∤ B_m B_j.

- The PDF could not be fetched, so the exact theorem numbers and whether the paper explicitly states an integer-coefficient identity were not verified. Before citing, check §6 (IBDWT) and the DWT convolution theorem (§3–4) in the original.

## Q3. fiat-crypto (Erbsen, Philipoom, Gross, Sloan, Chlipala, IEEE S&P 2019): weights, product coefficients, non-power-of-two and non-product-closed weights

### Takeaway
fiat-crypto's generic Coq library is the closest formal prior work.
- It works over an **arbitrary integer weight function** `weight : nat -> Z`. The core positional lemmas need only `weight 0 = 1` and nonzero weights.
- It multiplies in "associational" form: lists of (weight, coefficient) pairs, with the product weight equal to the product of weights.
- It converts back with `place`, which puts a term of weight w into the highest limb i whose weight divides w, with coefficient **w / weight i**. Applied to w = B_m B_j and i = m + j, this is exactly κ(m,j) = B_m B_j / B_{m+j}.
- `reduce s c` moves every term whose weight is divisible by s = B to weight w/s times c. This is the wrap gain.

So (b) and (c) are formally verified in fiat-crypto, in a different (associational) packaging. For non-product-closed weights fiat-crypto is still correct, but only by **exact** placement: the term falls to a lower limb with a bigger coefficient, or ultimately to limb 0. It never uses the congruence mod B − c to land a term. The landing criterion (d) is therefore not in fiat-crypto.

### Cited Findings (checked against source unless noted)
- **Paper.** A. Erbsen, J. Philipoom, J. Gross, R. Sloan, A. Chlipala, "Simple High-Level Code For Cryptographic Arithmetic – With Proofs, Without Compromises", IEEE S&P 2019, pp. 1202–1219.
  - Describes base 2^25.5 (alternating 26/25-bit limbs) as "mixed-radix".
  - Fig. 5 gives associational-to-positional conversion "including the fixing of misaligned weights" via a helper `place`. Given a weight and value, `place` "searches for the highest-index output position whose weight the given weight is a multiple of".
  - — [Chlipala lab PDF](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FiatCryptoSP19/FiatCryptoSP19.pdf); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/131080/fiat-crypto.pdf); [WireGuard mirror](https://www.wireguard.com/papers/erbsen-philipoom-gross-sloan-chlipala-fiat-2019.pdf)
- **`Associational.mul`.** Multiplies pairs `(fst t * fst t', snd t * snd t')`, i.e., weight × weight. `eval_mul : eval (mul p q) = eval p * eval q`. — [Core.v lines ~53–58](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/Core.v)
- **`Associational.split s p` and `reduce s c p`.**
  - `split` partitions terms on `fst t mod s =? 0` and divides the high part's weights by s.
  - `reduce s c p := lo ++ mul c hi`.
  - `eval_reduce : eval (reduce s c p) mod (s - eval c) = eval p mod (s - eval c)`.
  - c is itself an associational list, so multi-term Solinas-style c is allowed.
  - `eval_reduce_adjusted` handles a weight w with `w mod s = 0` and `c' = (w/s)·c`.
  - — [Core.v ~114–160](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/Core.v)
- **`Positional.place` (exact text).** Iterates i downward. If `fst t mod weight i =? 0`, it returns `(i, (fst t / weight i) * snd t)`, else it continues; at i = 0 it returns `(0, fst t * snd t)`.
  - Lemma `weight_place : weight (fst (place t i)) * snd (place t i) = fst t * snd t`. This is exact integer equality, not modular.
  - Lemma `place_weight` gives `place (weight i, x) n = (min i n, (weight i / weight (min i n)) * x)` under weight-monotone and divisibility hypotheses.
  - — [Core.v ~791–870](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/Core.v)
- **Hypotheses used.** `eval_from_associational`, `eval_mulmod`, `eval_carry` and `eval_carry_reduce` are proved `using weight_0 weight_nz` only. That means arbitrary nonzero integer weights: neither powers of two nor divisibility chains are required.
  - The stronger `weight_properties` record adds positivity, `weight (S i) mod weight i = 0` and `0 < weight (S i)/weight i`. It is needed for the carry/division lemmas and the generated code.
  - — [Core.v ~640–700, ~1230–1246](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/Core.v)
- **`Positional.mulmod`.** Defined as `from_associational n (repeat_reduce n s c (Associational.mul a b))`, with `eval_mulmod : eval (mulmod a b) mod (s - eval c) = (eval a * eval b) mod (s - eval c)`. — [Core.v ~920–931](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/Core.v)
- **Code-generation weight.** `ModOps.v` instantiates `weight i := 2^(-(-(limbwidth_num * i) / limbwidth_den))`, i.e., 2^{⌈(num/den)·i⌉}, proven equal to `2^Qceiling(limbwidth*i)`. This is the same ⌈qj/N⌉ schedule as Crandall–Fagin, and it is power-of-two only. — [ModOps.v ~31–60](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/ModOps.v)
- **Slides.** "Handling mixed-radix representations in proofs" with the example of placing a (500, x) term into the 100s slot (coefficient 5). — [Erbsen slides](https://www.ieee-security.org/TC/SP2019/SP19-Slides-pdfs/Andres_Erbsen_-_14-Andres_Erbsen-Simple_High-Level_Code_For_Cryptographic_Arithmetic_-_With_Proofs_Without_Compromises.pdf)
- **Deployment.** fiat-crypto's 2^255−19 code in BoringSSL is "unsaturated arithmetic modulo 2^255-19 using 10 limbs of radix 2^25.5"; only the prime is trusted, and limb parameters are compiler hints. — [BoringSSL third_party/fiat README](https://boringssl.googlesource.com/boringssl.git/+show/9770532afa91dd1441ba0d3e9d4bb86d7e501f19/third_party/fiat/README.md)

### Inferences
- **Product coefficients.** fiat-crypto does not *state* "κ(m,j) = B_m B_j / B_{m+j}" as a named theorem or table. It *computes* exactly that number, `fst t / weight i` with `fst t = weight m * weight j` and `i = m + j`, whenever weight (m+j) divides the product. That is the operational content of (c). The "product-closed" hypothesis is what makes `place` land on index m + j rather than a lower index.
  - fiat-crypto does not isolate this as a ring-level property. It is discharged per instance, or avoided because `place` is correct regardless.
- **Non-power-of-two weights.** The Core library is generic over integer weights, so e.g. radices (2, 3, 2) are expressible and `eval_mulmod` holds for them. The verified code generator (ModOps) uses only 2^{⌈·⌉} weights.
- **Non-product-closed weights.**
  - fiat-crypto remains *correct* but changes *shape*: a product term whose weight is not divisible by B_{m+j} falls to a lower index i with B_i | B_m B_j (at worst index 0, coefficient B_m B_j).
  - Wrap reduction `split s` only fires when B divides the weight. A non-divisible high term is never wrapped; it is just placed low with a huge coefficient.
  - fiat-crypto has no notion of placing a term at index k "modulo B − c" when B_k ∤ B_m B_j. That is precisely what the landing criterion gcd(εB_k, |B − c|) | B_m B_j addresses.
  - So (d) goes beyond fiat-crypto.
- **Proof assistant.** fiat-crypto is Coq (now Rocq), not Lean. I found no Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of positional unsaturated-limb modular arithmetic; see Q6.

- Did not inspect fiat-crypto's other Arithmetic files (e.g., `Freeze`, `Saturated`, `BaseConversion`, `WordByWordMontgomery`) for any κ-like lemma. The directory listing via the GitHub API was not retrieved.

## Q4. Bernstein's Curve25519 (radix 2^25.5, factors 19 / 2 / 38) and related crypto representations (Granger–Scott, Chung–Hasan, Solinas, AMNS/PMNS)

### Takeaway
The 25519 schoolbook gains are standard implementation knowledge:
- 19 on wrap;
- an extra factor 2 when both limb indices are odd, because ⌈25.5m⌉ + ⌈25.5j⌉ = ⌈25.5(m+j)⌉ + 1 exactly when m and j are both odd;
- hence 38 = 2·19.

They originate with Bernstein (2006), appear in ref10/donna code, and are machine-generated by fiat-crypto. The polynomial-ring view (residues as polynomials mod X^N − λ, or more general E(X), evaluated at a radix or at a root γ mod p) is the AMNS/PMNS line of Bajard–Imbert–Plantard. It covers (b) for general λ, but with a *uniform* evaluation point γ rather than a mixed schedule.

### Cited Findings
- **Sandy2x.** Describes the radix-2^25.5 layout h_0 + 2^26 h_1 + 2^51 h_2 + ... mod 2^255 − 19 with alternating 26/25-bit limbs, and the ×19 wrap. Notes that limbs must be multiplied by 19 and how 19s is computed by shift/subtract. — [T. Chou, "Sandy2x: New Curve25519 Speed Records", SAC 2015, ePrint 2015/943](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/943.pdf); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-31301-6_8)
- **Overview.** Arithmetic mod 2^255 − 19: digit-10 overflow folded back as ×19 into digit 0. — [J. D. Cook blog](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2019/03/09/curve25519/)
- **[recalled, not verified this session]** D. J. Bernstein, "Curve25519: new Diffie-Hellman speed records", PKC 2006, LNCS 3958, pp. 207–228 ([cr.yp.to/ecdh/curve25519-20060209.pdf](https://cr.yp.to/ecdh/curve25519-20060209.pdf)).
  - Represents field elements as Σ u_i 2^{⌈25.5i⌉}, i = 0..9, and computes products as polynomial products followed by reduction using 2^255 ≡ 19.
  - The "×2 for odd–odd index pairs" rule is visible in ref10 `fe_mul.c`, which precomputes `f1_2 = 2*f1`, `g1_19 = 19*g1` and uses 38-multiples. I could not fetch the paper to quote the section.
- **[recalled, not verified this session]** Granger & Scott, "Faster ECC over F_{2^521−1}", PKC 2015, LNCS 9020. — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-662-46447-2_24.pdf)
  - I recall they represent residues in base t = 2^58 with 9 limbs, so t^9 = 2^522 ≡ 2 (mod p): a wrap gain c = 2 with B − c = 2^522 − 2 = 2p.
  - That would be a real-world non-coprime wrap gain (gcd(c, B) = 2) relevant to (d). Verify before citing.
- **Granger & Moss.** "Generalised Mersenne Numbers Revisited", *Math. Comp.* 82 (2013) 2389–2420. Propose a different generalisation of Mersenne numbers (cyclotomic / generalised-repunit primes) for which residue multiplication is as efficient relative to integer multiplication as for Mersenne numbers. — [arXiv:1108.3054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.3054); [ePrint 2011/444](https://eprint.iacr.org/2011/444); [AMS](https://www.ams.org/journals/mcom/2013-82-284/S0025-5718-2013-02704-4)
- **MNS / AMNS.** Bajard, Imbert, Plantard, "Modular Number Systems: Beyond the Mersenne Family", LIRMM report 04006 / SAC 2004.
  - Introduces the MNS and the AMNS. Integers mod p are represented as polynomials evaluated at γ with γ^n ≡ λ (mod p), i.e., E(X) = X^n − λ. Positioned explicitly against Chung–Hasan's "more generalized Mersenne numbers" (SAC 2003).
  - — [HAL lirmm-00109208](https://hal-lirmm.ccsd.cnrs.fr/lirmm-00109208/document); [Didier, Dosso, Véron, arXiv:1901.11485 (generates multiple AMNS with E(X) = X^n − λ)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.11485)
  - The polynomial modular number system (PMNS) generalisation over Z/pZ: [Bajard, Marrez, Plantard, Véron, arXiv:2001.03741](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.03741)
  - Lagrange-representation AMNS multiplication: [Negre & Plantard, ACISP 2008](https://ro.uow.edu.au/infopapers/3194)
  - Practical example E = X^4 − 2 for a ~64-bit p: [Noyez, Mrabet, Potin, Véron, SAC 2024 slides](https://www.sacworkshop.org/SAC24/slides/NoyezMrabetPotinVeron_talk.pdf)
- **[recalled]** J. Chung, M. A. Hasan, "More Generalized Mersenne Numbers", SAC 2003, LNCS 3006, pp. 335–347. Primes p = f(t) with t near the word size; modular multiplication is polynomial multiplication mod f followed by base-t evaluation.
- **Unsaturated-limb practice.** NCC Group (T. Pornin) "On multiplications with unsaturated limbs" is a practitioner discussion (not read). — [NCC Group](https://www.nccgroup.com/research/on-multiplications-with-unsaturated-limbs/)

### Inferences
- **AMNS/PMNS is the closest *mathematical* framework to (b).** Residues are elements of Z[X]/(E(X)) mapped to Z/p by X ↦ γ. The user's ring is the mixed-radix analogue: X^N = c on the *radix* side, with B − c playing the role of p (or a multiple of it).
- **Difference from AMNS.** AMNS uses a uniform evaluation point γ (all κ = 1). The user's mixed rings have κ(m,j) ≠ 1. In AMNS terms, 25519 radix-2^25.5 is not an AMNS: it is an "irrational γ = 2^25.5" system, which is exactly what κ = 2 compensates for.
- **The 19/2/38 gains are textbook implementation facts.** Deriving them from a general κ-formula and proving it formally (fiat-crypto does so implicitly, in Coq) is not new in substance. Presenting κ(m,j) = B_m B_j / B_{m+j} as a closed form on any product-closed schedule is a clean general statement, but should be framed as a generalisation/formalization, not a discovery.

## Q5. Is multiplication on non-product-closed mixed-radix representations (B_m B_j not a multiple of B_{m+j}) studied anywhere? Is the "landing criterion" / "residue-closed" notion present?

### Takeaway
No source found studies multiplication on mixed-radix wrap-around representations where B_{m+j} ∤ B_m B_j, gives a solvability criterion for landing a product term on a target limb modulo B − c, or uses the phrase "residue-closed". The criterion is an application of the standard linear-congruence theorem: a·x ≡ b (mod m) is solvable iff gcd(a, m) | b. Its use for limb placement appears to be new.

### Cited Findings
- fiat-crypto's `place` handles non-divisible weights only by exact fallback to a lower index with coefficient `w / weight i`, ending at index 0 with coefficient w. `split s` only wraps terms whose weight is divisible by s. — [Core.v](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/Core.v)
- Crandall–Fagin's and fiat-crypto's practical schedules are 2^{⌈qj/N⌉}, which is always product-closed, since ⌈a⌉ + ⌈b⌉ ≥ ⌈a+b⌉. — [ModOps.v](https://github.com/mit-plv/fiat-crypto/blob/master/src/Arithmetic/ModOps.v); [Wikipedia IBDWT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_base_discrete_weighted_transform)
- Searches combining chip-firing/number-system, mixed-radix multiplication and fiat-crypto weight terms returned nothing on non-product-closed multiplication. Search queries are listed in the Gaps below.

### Inferences
- **(d) is the most likely genuinely novel mathematical content.** In particular:
  - the criterion gcd(ε·B_k, |B − c|) | B_m B_j for "landing" B_m B_j at place k modulo B − c (ε = c on wrap, else 1);
  - the observation that a wrap gain coprime to |B − c| (more precisely, making every ε·B_k invertible mod |B − c|) makes every schedule residue-closed.
- Even so, it is a short corollary of a classical fact, so it should be presented as a small, useful lemma, not a deep theorem.
- One relevant check: when gcd(c, B) > 1 (e.g., the recalled Granger–Scott c = 2 with B = 2^522), B − c is even and the wrap-gain coprimality fails. Real systems then work mod p = (B − c)/2 rather than mod B − c, which the landing criterion as stated (mod B − c) would not directly cover.
- Knuth TAOCP vol. 2 (§4.1 positional number systems, mixed-radix notation; §4.3 multiple-precision arithmetic) treats mixed radix for representation and conversion **[recalled]**. I am not aware of a mixed-radix multiplication algorithm there, and could not verify this.
- Factorial-number-system arithmetic: no source found on multiplication in factoradic with wrap. The factoradic schedule is not product-closed (B_m B_j = m! j! is generally not divisible by (m+j)!).
